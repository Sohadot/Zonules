Focus Integrity Engine

One shared framework. Layer-specific criteria. Traceable failure classifications.

The Focus Integrity Assessment applies the same standard to the eye, to perception, and to machine vision — literally in anatomy, by analogy in perception, as a technical vocabulary in machine vision. You supply structured judgements; the engine applies fixed rules and maps each finding to the ontology class and reference units that explain it.

CASE-A · Zonular weakness and lens-support instability

L1 The biological eye · Source-derived educational scenario · literal application (anatomical fact)

Context
An educational scenario drawn from standard ocular-anatomy references: the zonular fibers are weakened or partly ruptured, so the crystalline lens is no longer held in even, centred tension and can tilt or shift (described clinically as ectopia lentis). The lens itself is assumed clear.
Evidence
SRC-001 — Forrester JV, Dick AD, McMenamin PG, Roberts F, Pearlman E. The Eye: Basic Sciences in Practice.; SRC-002 — Standring S (ed.). Gray's Anatomy: The Anatomical Basis of Clinical Practice.; SRC-003 — American Academy of Ophthalmology — Basic and Clinical Science Course (BCSC), Section on Fundamentals and Principles of Ophthalmology.
Structured inputs
  • FIS-1 Suspension — failed
  • FIS-2 Accommodation — at risk
  • FIS-3 Separation — intact
  • FIS-4 Provenance — not applicable at this layer
  • FIS-5 Interpretation — not applicable at this layer
Applicable criteria
FIS-1, FIS-2, FIS-3
Resulting classification
Focus integrity: compromised — FIO-01 Suspension Failure (failed) · FIO-02 Accommodation Failure (at risk)
Interpretation
Suspension Failure (FIO-01) is the primary class: the focal element is present but its holding structure has given way. Because accommodation depends on tension transmitted through the zonules, the adjustable range is recorded as at risk (FIO-02). Provenance and interpretation are not applicable at the anatomical layer and do not affect the reading.
Limitations
Educational illustration only. It is not a clinical case, does not describe any individual, and is not a diagnosis. Real zonular disorders vary in cause and severity and require examination by a qualified eye-care professional. Lens clarity (FIS-3) is set by the scenario, not inferred.
Reference units
/zonules-of-zinn/ · /crystalline-lens/ · /lens-accommodation/ · /ciliary-body/

CASE-B · The reversible vase–faces figure

L2 Human perception · Published empirical phenomenon · conceptual analogy

Context
Rubin's reversible figure can be seen either as a vase or as two facing profiles. The image does not change; the assignment of figure and ground alternates. The phenomenon is documented in standard perception-science references.
Evidence
SRC-006 — Palmer SE. Vision Science: Photons to Phenomenology.; SRC-005 — Goldstein EB, Cacciamani L. Sensation and Perception.
Structured inputs
  • FIS-1 Suspension — intact
  • FIS-2 Accommodation — not assessed
  • FIS-3 Separation — at risk
  • FIS-4 Provenance — intact
  • FIS-5 Interpretation — at risk
Applicable criteria
FIS-1, FIS-2, FIS-3, FIS-4, FIS-5
Resulting classification
Focus integrity: at risk — FIO-03 Signal–Noise Failure (at risk) · FIO-05 Interpretation Failure (at risk)
Interpretation
Figure–ground separation is unstable (FIO-03, at risk) and two interpretations compete (FIO-05, at risk). Nothing is marked failed: the visual system is behaving normally in response to genuinely ambiguous input. Perceptual constancy (FIS-2) is not tested by this figure and is reported as not assessed rather than intact.
Limitations
The perceptual phenomenon is documented; its mapping to FIO classes is a conceptual analogy within the Zonules.com framework, not a finding of perception science. Reversible figures are not perceptual disorders.
Reference units
/figure-ground/ · /bistable-perception/ · /gestalt-principles/ · /visual-perception/

CASE-C · An adversarial perturbation causes a confident misclassification

L3 A machine vision system · Published empirical phenomenon · technical application

Context
Goodfellow, Shlens and Szegedy (2015) showed that adding a small, visually imperceptible perturbation to an image correctly classified as a panda caused an image classifier to label it a gibbon with high confidence. The example is reproduced in the standard deep-learning reference text.
Evidence
SRC-028 — Goodfellow IJ, Shlens J, Szegedy C. Explaining and Harnessing Adversarial Examples. International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR), 2015.; SRC-017 — Goodfellow I, Bengio Y, Courville A. Deep Learning.
Structured inputs
  • FIS-1 Suspension — at risk
  • FIS-2 Accommodation — not assessed
  • FIS-3 Separation — failed
  • FIS-4 Provenance — not assessed
  • FIS-5 Interpretation — failed
Applicable criteria
FIS-1, FIS-2, FIS-3, FIS-4, FIS-5
Resulting classification
Focus integrity: compromised — FIO-01 Suspension Failure (at risk) · FIO-03 Signal–Noise Failure (failed) · FIO-05 Interpretation Failure (failed)
Interpretation
Separation from adversarial noise fails (FIO-03) and the output label is wrong (FIO-05). High confidence in a wrong label indicates that confidence did not track correctness, recorded as a calibration risk (FIO-01, at risk). Robustness to operating conditions and input provenance were not part of the published demonstration and are reported as not assessed.
Limitations
The classification restates a published result in FIO vocabulary; it is not a new experiment, benchmark, or performance claim. The engine did not analyse any image. Provenance is a separate property: a perfectly provenanced image can still be adversarially perturbed, and an unperturbed image can lack provenance.
Reference units
/adversarial-examples/ · /model-calibration/ · /robustness/ · /image-classification/

What this engine is — and is not

A deterministic, rule-based classifier. It maps structured judgements that you supply (intact, at risk, failed) onto the Focus Integrity Standard (FIS) and returns the corresponding Focus Integrity Ontology (FIO) classes with links to the reference units that explain them.

It is not:

  • an independent scientific validator — it does not verify whether your inputs are true
  • an automated authenticity or deepfake detector — it inspects no image or media
  • a medical or diagnostic tool — it assesses no individual's eyes
  • a machine-learning inference service — it contains no model and makes no network call
  • a replacement for qualified, domain-specific evaluation

The Focus Integrity Assessment

The Focus Integrity Standard defines five criteria. Each layer applies the criteria within its scope: all five in perception and machine vision, three in anatomy, where provenance and interpretation are not anatomical properties. Each failed or at-risk criterion maps to one class in the Focus Integrity Ontology, and each class resolves to governed reference pages. The interactive reading enhances this framework; it does not replace it.

The five criteria (FIS)

  • FIS-1 · Suspension. The structures holding the focal element are identified and stable.
  • FIS-2 · Accommodation. The system adjusts focus across its intended operating range.
  • FIS-3 · Separation. The system separates signal from noise within stated tolerances.
  • FIS-4 · Provenance. The system can establish the origin or authenticity of what it sees to a stated confidence.
  • FIS-5 · Interpretation. Held, clean focus yields correct interpretation, not merely sharp signal.

The five failure classes (FIO)

  • FIO-01 · Suspension Failure. When the suspension layer fails, the focal element is present but unstable.
  • FIO-02 · Accommodation Failure. A system that focuses at one setting only does not have focus integrity.
  • FIO-03 · Signal–Noise Failure. Focus without separation produces sharp noise, not meaning.
  • FIO-04 · Provenance Failure. In an age of synthetic images, focus without provenance cannot yield visual truth.
  • FIO-05 · Interpretation Failure. The final failure of focus is not blur — it is confident misreading.

Reference units: Zonules of Zinn · Ciliary Body · Crystalline Lens · Lens Capsule · Lens Accommodation · Visual Perception · Machine Vision.
